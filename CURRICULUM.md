# Czech A1 → B2 Curriculum Roadmap

Goal: go from zero Czech to passing the **CCE–B2** exam (Certifikovaná zkouška z češtiny pro cizince), which is accepted as equivalent to the Czech citizenship/permanent-residency language exam.

This roadmap sequences what to learn and in what order. The app will follow this structure: each level is a set of short **lessons** (vocab + one grammar point + a few easy check questions), and only B2 unlocks the full CCE-B2-style exam practice (reading passages, matching, transformation, timed drills) you already saw.

Czech grammar note up front: the hard part of Czech isn't vocabulary, it's the **case system** (7 grammatical cases, each changing noun/adjective/pronoun endings depending on gender) and **verb aspect** (every verb has a "doing" form and a "done" form). This roadmap deliberately delays cases beyond nominative until A2, because trying to learn them alongside basic vocabulary is what makes most beginners quit.

---

## A1 — Survival Czech (~10–12 lessons)

Goal: introduce yourself, handle basic transactions, be understood on essentials. No cases beyond nominative; verbs only in present tense.

1. **Pronunciation & alphabet** — Czech-specific letters (ř, ě, ů, ý, háček/čárka), why spelling is very phonetic (a real advantage over English).
2. **Greetings & introductions** — dobrý den, ahoj, jak se máte, jmenuji se..., odkud jste.
3. **Numbers 0–100, time & dates** — telling time, days of the week, months.
4. **Být and mít (to be / to have)** — the two most-used irregular verbs, present tense, all persons.
5. **Basic word order & questions** — yes/no questions, question words (co, kdo, kde, kdy, jak, proč).
6. **Nouns & gender (nominative only)** — masculine/feminine/neuter, why it matters, how to spot gender from the word ending.
7. **Adjectives (nominative agreement)** — matching adjective endings to noun gender.
8. **Family & people** — vocabulary + possessives (můj, tvůj, jeho, její).
9. **Food & ordering** — restaurant/café phrases, chtěl bych, prosím, děkuji.
10. **Shopping & numbers in context** — prices, kolik to stojí, basic negotiation phrases.
11. **Directions & places in town** — kde je..., doleva/doprava/rovně, basic prepositions of place.
12. **A1 review & mini-check** — cumulative light quiz across all of the above (still easy, no exam pressure).

## A2 — Everyday Czech (~12–14 lessons)

Goal: handle daily situations, past and future tense, start using cases for real sentences.

1. **Accusative case** — the "direct object" case; most frequently needed case after nominative.
2. **Genitive case** — possession, negation, quantities ("a lot of", "a little").
3. **Past tense** — l-participle formation, agreement with subject gender (this is where most learners' errors cluster — worth its own lesson).
4. **Verb aspect, part 1** — perfective vs. imperfective, why Czech has two verbs for one English verb (e.g., dělat / udělat), common aspect pairs.
5. **Future tense** — budu + infinitive (imperfective) vs. perfective present-form-as-future.
6. **Modal verbs** — muset, moct, chtít, smět and how they combine with infinitives.
7. **Weather & seasons**
8. **Hobbies & free time** — likes/dislikes, mít rád + accusative.
9. **Travel & transport** — buying tickets, asking about schedules, prepositions of motion.
10. **Health & body** — bolí mě..., basic pharmacy/doctor vocabulary.
11. **Housing & daily routine** — reflexive verbs (myslet se → myslím si, etc.), daily-routine time expressions.
12. **Locative case** — used after v/na/o + place and topic expressions ("about something").
13. **Instrumental case** — means/instrument, "with someone", after certain verbs (zabývat se, stát se).
14. **A2 review & mini-check.**

## B1 — Independent Czech (~12–14 lessons)

Goal: handle work/study situations, opinions, hypotheticals; cases become automatic.

1. **Dative case** — the last core case; indirect objects, giving/telling verbs.
2. **All 7 cases — consolidated review** — a single lesson mapping all cases side by side with a reference table (this is the payoff lesson after 2 levels of building them one at a time).
3. **Conditional mood** — kdybych, bych, hypotheticals ("if I had...").
4. **Verb aspect, part 2** — aspect in the imperative, in narration, common irregular pairs.
5. **Comparatives & superlatives**
6. **Expressing opinions & agreement/disagreement** — myslím, že..., souhlasím/nesouhlasím.
7. **Work & studies vocabulary** — job interviews, CVs, school system basics.
8. **Reported speech** — říkal, že...
9. **Connecting ideas** — conjunctions beyond a/ale (přestože, i když, proto, takže) — needed for both writing and listening comprehension at B1+.
10. **Idiomatic & phrasal expressions** — common fixed phrases that don't translate literally.
11. **Reading short authentic texts** — news snippets, simple articles, first exposure to the CCE reading task *style* (multiple choice, still short texts).
12. **Listening to natural speech** — slower authentic audio (interviews, announcements).
13. **Formal vs. informal register** — ty vs. vy, formal writing conventions (needed for B2 Writing subtest).
14. **B1 review & mini-check.**

## B2 — Exam-Ready Czech (~10 lessons + full exam-prep bank)

Goal: everything from here maps directly onto the 5 CCE–B2 subtests you already saw structured in the app.

1. **Advanced syntax & cohesion devices** — the connectors/pronoun-reference skills specifically tested in Grammar Úloha 5 and Writing.
2. **Word formation & derivation** — prefixes/suffixes, needed for Grammar Úloha 3–4 (gap-fill, transformation tasks).
3. **Abstract & professional topics vocabulary** — the register used in CCE reading passages (society, environment, technology, culture).
4. **Reading strategy: matching & gap-in-text** — the specific technique for Úloha 1/3 (reconstructing text order, matching titles).
5. **Reading strategy: true/false & implicit meaning** — Úloha 4 technique, spotting when a statement is *not stated* vs. *contradicted*.
6. **Listening strategy** (once audio is added) — note-taking during a single playback, since each recording plays only twice.
7. **Formal letter/email writing** — Psaní Úloha 1 structure and the 4-point rubric.
8. **Article/essay writing** — Psaní Úloha 2, choosing between article/úvaha/recenze.
9. **Speaking prep** — describing an experience, summarizing a text, discussion phrases (once speaking practice is added).
10. **Full-length timed mock exam** — this is where the CCE-B2-style content you already saw (reading passages, matching, true/false, grammar drills) becomes the main event.

## How this maps to the app

- **Learn track** (new, to be built next): A1 → B2 lessons above, each = vocab cards + one short grammar note + 3–5 easy check questions. Untimed, low-pressure, unlocks sequentially.
- **Exam-prep track** (already built): the CCE-B2-style reading/grammar/vocab items — reframed as the *destination*, reachable once B1/B2 lessons are underway, not the front door.
- **Writing reference cards** (already built): stay tied to the B2 Writing lessons above.

## Suggested pace

Roughly 2–3 lessons/week with regular review is a realistic pace for A1→B2 self-study — that puts B2-readiness at roughly 8–10 months, in line with typical CEFR level-to-level estimates (~150–200 contact hours per level for a language as grammatically different from English as Czech).
